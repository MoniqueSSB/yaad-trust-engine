The payment journey

Where your money goes,
every step of it.

Most people building back home have no idea where their money sits between sending it and the work being done. That gap is where it goes missing. So here is the whole route, written down before you part with anything.

💷
Standing behind the work until it is proven is what Yaadly is built to do. It is not switched on yet. We are waiting on legal sign off and insurance, and we will not take payment for anybody's job until both are in hand. When they land, we will say so on this page.

So here is how a job runs in the meantime. You pay your tradesperson directly, stage by stage, against an agreement you both sign before a tool is lifted. Nothing passes through us during this period, so we cannot pay him, hold payment back, or hand anything to you, and we will not pretend otherwise.

What you get today, and it is not nothing. The tradesperson vetted before they reach you. The agreement written down. The evidence held to our standard, before, during and after, timestamped. A free dispute review by a person. Somebody to call who answers. We invoice our own fee, and only our own fee, after the work is done.

Until then, if anyone sends you a Yaadly payment link for a tradesperson's labour or materials, it did not come from us.

The rule above every stage

Never more than a quarter, before anything exists

Consumer guidance in the UK is consistent and we follow it: never a deposit above 25%, ideally nothing up front, or only enough to cover materials. Stage payments tied to milestones you can see, not to dates. Never the final instalment until you have inspected the work.

📐
Materials at cost are the one exemption, and it is the guidance's own. Materials are a pass-through, not a deposit, and no fee is ever charged on them. So on a small job the materials stage can be more than a quarter of the total, and that is allowed. We say so on your quote rather than leaving you to count it.
🧱
If there is nothing to buy, we do not take a deposit. A one or two day job with no materials is paid in full on completion, nothing up front at all. That is what the trade does when it is done honestly, and asking for money on a one day job is a warning sign, not a standard.

Naming a stage

If you cannot photograph it, it is not a stage

"50% complete" and "after two weeks" are not triggers, they are guesses. Every trade has real ones: strip out, first fix, plaster, second fix, snagging. On a roof it is materials delivered, old roof stripped and deck exposed, membrane down, zinc fixed and flashing sealed, site cleared and filmed. Written in the trade's own words, agreed before the job, one photograph each.

Materials

Who carries a loss, and when

Your tradesperson buys the materials and you pay for them first, so he is never funding your job out of his own pocket. You nominate and approve where they are stored overnight. A lockable room, the garage, a container. If there is nowhere securable, say so before the job starts: they buy in smaller drops and clear surplus nightly, and that is priced in rather than discovered after a theft.

🌗
Recorded last thing every day he attends, and again first thing next morning, in the place you approved. Those two timestamps decide who carries a loss, so it is never an argument about what probably happened. Gone during the working day, while they are in his hands: his loss. Gone overnight, from the place you approved, with the evening record showing them stored correctly: yours, because they were locked on your property with him not there.
📵
No evening record, no transfer. The risk stays with him. The record is the thing that moves it. His tools are always his own risk, day or night, and never yours.
⚖️
If he was paid for materials and cannot produce them, or the loss looks deliberate, a person reviews it, and only a human finding of malice goes on his record.
🛡️
Check your insurance covers materials on site. Cover on an unoccupied property is usually the most restricted there is. Yaadly does not insure materials.

Say the quiet part first

What these services are

A record of physical works and costs by a construction project manager: what was observed on site on the date stated, and how your quoted prices compare against real material costs, day rates and completed jobs.

What they are not

Not a law firm, not legal advice. No title checks and no reading of agreements, that is an attorney's work. No land surveys or boundaries (Land Surveyors Act) and no certifying structural adequacy (PERB). No commission on any property transaction.

What we owe you

Reasonable care and skill, as section 49 of the Consumer Rights Act 2015 requires. Nothing in our terms limits that.

On the site, in every quote, and inside the report itself.

⚖️
Why every price on this site is all-in. Under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 a mandatory charge must sit inside the displayed price. The CMA fined the AA £4.2m over a £3 booking fee shown separately. Presentation is the trigger, not scale.
