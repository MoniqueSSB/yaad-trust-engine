How we use AI

Assistants draft.
A named person decides.

Yaadly runs on a small set of assistants. They read your message at two in the morning, scope the job, draft the paperwork and check that the photographs arrived. That is why you get an answer straight away and a person replies within one working day, rather than waiting for both.

They do not decide anything. A named person at Yaadly approves every stage, issues every document, sets every price and signs off every job. Releasing money, ruling on a disagreement and changing a tradesperson's record are decisions no assistant here can make.

That is not a policy we ask them to follow. It is built into the system, in the database and in the code, so it cannot be talked around by anyone typing the right thing into a chat window. Five of those limits are written out further down, and each one is checkable rather than a promise.

Why this matters to you

What you get out of it, in three lines.

You are managing a property from four or five time zones away. Most of what goes wrong at that distance is not bad work. It is silence, and not knowing who is answerable for what.

An answer while Jamaica is asleep

Send a message, a photograph or a voice note at any hour, in Patois if that is easier, and it is read and answered there and then. A person comes back to you within one working day. You are never left wondering whether it arrived.

A written verdict in days

Drafting the findings inside a report is the slow part, and a machine does that part. The ratings and the verdict on page one are written by a person. That is how a report lands normally within 72 hours of the visit instead of whenever somebody finds an evening.

A name against every decision

Every step that moves money or changes what happens at your property has a real person's name recorded against it. Not "the system", not "our team". When you ask who approved something, there is an answer, and it is a person you can speak to.

Where the line sits

What happens by machine, and what waits for a person.

Straight away, by machine: your message is read and answered, a voice note is turned into text, the job is written up in a structured form, a tradesperson's paperwork is read and anything missing is flagged, the quote brief and the written agreement are drafted, photographs and walkthrough video are read and what is visible in them is written up, the findings inside a paid report are drafted, and a job that has gone quiet is noticed and chased.
Always by a named person: approving a stage, which is the step that moves money. Issuing every document. Setting every price. Deciding a disagreement. Changing a tradesperson's record. Accepting or declining somebody who has applied to work with us. Judging whether work meets the standard for that trade. Rating how serious a finding is, and writing the verdict on page one of a report you paid for.
One gate is not ours, it is the tradesperson's. Before a written account of a day's work reaches you, the person who did that work sees it first, framed honestly as a draft, and chooses whether it goes to you as written or in their own words instead. He did the work, so it is his account to stand behind, not something assembled about him while he was not looking.

That cuts both ways on purpose. A protection that only ever guards the client is telling the tradesperson what we think of him, and a tradesperson who does not trust the process is not one you want on your property.

Five things we built rather than promised

Anyone can say a human is in charge.

So here is what that is worth. Each of these is a limit built into the code and the database, not a rule we ask an assistant to remember.

Our invoicing assistant cannot type a number. It can name a service from our published price list and nothing else. There is no field in it for an amount. The figure is looked up from the list itself when the invoice is written, so an assistant that wanted to invent a price has nowhere to put one. An invoice with anything on it we could not match to the published list cannot be sent at all until a person prices that line.
Our property sketches carry no measurements. A phone video has no scale in it, so any dimension taken from one would be invented, and measured drawings for reward are regulated work in Jamaica. The rule is enforced three times over: the assistant is told not to, anything that slips through is stripped out and reported to us rather than hidden, and the database refuses to let a pack carrying a measurement be approved. A sketch shows you which rooms there are and how they connect. It never tells you how big they are.
Our report drafter cannot tell you how serious anything is. Three of the services you can buy are a document: the Deposit Protection Check, the Condition Report and the Technical Sign-off. A machine drafts the findings inside them, which is a large part of why a report reaches you in days rather than weeks. It is not allowed to rate a single one of those findings, and there is no field in it for a verdict.

Severe, moderate or low is the professional judgment you are paying for, and a named person writes it, along with the conclusion on page one. The database will not let a report be issued while a finding is unrated, while the verdict is empty, or if a measurement has crept in anywhere. So the part of a report that a machine cannot do is exactly the part you bought.
Identity documents never reach an AI model. A tradesperson's government photo ID, their live selfie and their face video stay where they were uploaded. They are held back before the file is even fetched, and there is deliberately no setting that turns that off. The identity check itself is done by a specialist provider built for it, not by us guessing from a photograph.

Their supporting paperwork, meaning proof of address, tax registration, certificates, CV and portfolio, is read by a model only if they said yes, and no is what we assume if they did not answer. That choice is recorded with the exact sentence they agreed to, so a consent can never quietly come to mean something it did not say when they gave it.
A model reads your photographs, and we would rather say so than let you find out. Photographs and walkthrough video of your property are analysed by a model run by NVIDIA, in the United States. It writes down what is visible and tags anything it notices as cosmetic, maintenance, safety, structural, or not what was asked for. That is useful, and it is also a machine looking at video of your house, so it is named here and on the privacy page with the country it sits in.

What it thinks does not reach you unread. The message that lands on your phone when photographs arrive carries what is visible in plain words and nothing else. The severity it assigned, and any sentence about needing a professional, are taken out of your copy and sent to us instead, where a person reads them. A machine telling somebody overseas that their house may have a structural problem is a diagnosis, and we are not willing to have one made by something that has never stood in the building. Where that judgment does reach you it is inside a document a person here opened, read and released, never something sent while we slept. The tradesperson gets the unedited version, because he is standing in front of the thing and can go and look again.

The limits

What no assistant here will ever do.

Nothing is closed by a countdown. Not a timer, not a confidence score, not an evidence check going green on its own. No stage approves itself because the photographs looked right, and nothing moves because you did not reply in time. There is no setting that makes it work that way, and we are not going to add one, because it is the only thing that makes the rest of this worth anything.
Four questions never come to us at all, and never to a machine. Valuation goes to a licensed valuer. Title and legal opinion go to an attorney. Anything structural goes to a PERB registered engineer. Boundaries go to a commissioned land surveyor. We say so in writing and name the professional, rather than answering it ourselves because we were asked.

What Yaadly does guarantee is what Yaadly controls: project management, procurement and oversight judgment. We record condition and we judge workmanship against the standard for that trade. We do not guarantee an outcome. See the managed services page for what each service does and does not cover.

Your information

Where it goes, said plainly.

Every company that touches your information is named, with the country it is in, on our privacy page. That list is kept current rather than written once, and it includes the uncomfortable parts. Our text drafting model sat in China until 4 September 2026 and now runs in the European Union. The privacy page said so plainly for as long as it was true, and it still records that it was true, because a page that only describes the present tense is one you cannot check.

Not all of it is in Europe, and the list is the honest one rather than the flattering one. Text you send us is read and drafted on by Mistral, in the European Union. Photographs and video of your property are read by NVIDIA in the United States. Voice notes are turned into text by Cloudflare, with providers in the United States standing behind it when that fails. Identity documents go to none of them.

You can ask us not to use the assistants on your job. Say so and we will take them out of your thread and answer you ourselves. It will be slower, and that is a fair trade to offer you rather than a reason to talk you out of it.

We can switch every assistant off. There is one control that stops them, and when it is on your message is still recorded, your photographs and voice notes are still kept, and you are told a person is reading it. Nobody is left talking to silence.

Questions people ask

The ones that come up most.

Is a machine deciding whether my money is released?
No, and it is the one thing we will not build. Approving a stage is the step that releases money, and it is done by a named person every single time. Nothing releases on a timer, on an evidence check passing, or because an assistant judged itself confident enough.

Does AI set my price?
No. Prices come from our published list, and our invoicing assistant has no field in it for an amount. If a line of work is not on the published list, an invoice carrying it cannot be sent until a person prices it. See what work costs.

Who actually looks at photographs of my property?
A model run by NVIDIA, in the United States, reads them first and writes down what is visible. Then a person here reads it. What lands on your phone automatically is the plain description only. Any judgment about how serious something is comes to us first, and reaches you inside a document a person opened and released.

Can I ask you not to use AI on my job at all?
Yes. Say so and we take the assistants out of your thread and answer you ourselves. It will be slower, and we would rather tell you that than talk you out of asking.

What happens when the AI gets something wrong?
It gets caught at the gate, because the gates are where the consequences are. A wrong draft is a draft a person corrects before it goes anywhere. The decisions that would actually cost you, releasing money, ruling on a disagreement, rating a finding, are not the assistant's to make in the first place, so a mistake there cannot reach you as a decision.

Will I be talking to a bot instead of a person?
You will get an immediate answer from an assistant, and a person within one working day. If you ask to speak to somebody, you get Monique, replying in the same conversation from the same number rather than as a stranger messaging you out of nowhere.

Are tradespeople's identity documents fed into AI?
Never. The government photo ID, the live selfie and the face video are blocked in our code before the file is even fetched, and there is no setting that turns that off. A specialist identity provider does that check. Their other paperwork is read by a model only where they agreed to it, and an unanswered question counts as no.
