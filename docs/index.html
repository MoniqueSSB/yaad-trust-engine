The payment journey

Where your money goes,
every step of it.

Most people building back home have no idea where their money sits between sending it and the work being done. That gap is where it goes missing. So here is the whole route, written down before you part with anything.

💷
Holding your money until the work is proven is what Yaadly is built to do. It is not switched on yet. We are waiting on legal sign off and government approval, and we will not touch a penny of anybody's money until both are in hand. When they land, we will say so on this page.

So here is how a job runs in the meantime. You pay your tradesperson directly, stage by stage, against an agreement you both sign before a tool is lifted. Nothing passes through us during this period, so we cannot hold your money, release it, or hand it back, and we will not pretend otherwise.

What you get today, and it is not nothing. The tradesperson vetted before they reach you. The agreement written down. The evidence held to our standard, before, during and after, timestamped. A free dispute review by a person. Somebody to call who answers. We invoice our own fee, and only our own fee, after the work is done.

Until holding opens, if anyone sends you a Yaadly payment link for a tradesperson's labour or materials, it did not come from us.

The rule above every stage

Never more than a quarter, before anything exists

Consumer guidance in the UK is consistent and we follow it: never a deposit above 25%, ideally nothing up front, or only enough to cover materials. Stage payments tied to milestones you can see, not to dates. Never the final instalment until you have inspected the work.

📐
Materials at cost are the one exemption, and it is the guidance's own. Materials are a pass-through, not a deposit, and no fee is ever charged on them. So on a small job the materials stage can be more than a quarter of the total, and that is allowed. We say so on your quote rather than leaving you to count it.
🧱
If there is nothing to buy, we do not take a deposit. A one or two day job with no materials is paid in full on completion, nothing up front at all. That is what the trade does when it is done honestly, and asking for money on a one day job is a warning sign, not a standard.

If materials go missing

Who carries the loss, and the record that settles it

You nominate and approve where on the property the materials are stored overnight. A lockable room, the garage, a container. You approving it is what makes an overnight loss yours rather than their guess, so it is your call. If there is nowhere securable, say so before the job starts: they buy in smaller drops and clear surplus nightly, and that is priced in rather than discovered after a theft.

📸
Materials are recorded last thing every day the tradesperson attends, and again first thing next morning, in the place you approved. Those two timestamps decide who carries a loss, so it is never an argument about what probably happened.
☀️
Gone during the working day, while they are in the tradesperson's hands: their loss.
🌙
Gone overnight, from the place you approved, with the evening record showing them stored correctly: your loss, because they were locked on your property with the tradesperson not there.
🚫
No evening record, no transfer, and it stays theirs. The record is the thing that moves it. Their own tools are always their own risk, day or night.
⚖️
If they were paid for materials and cannot produce them, or the loss looks deliberate, a person reviews it, and only a human finding of malice goes on their record.
