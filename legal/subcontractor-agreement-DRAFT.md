# Yaadly tradesperson agreement

**DRAFT of 5 September 2026. Not for signature. Not published.**

This is the written agreement between Yaadly Ltd and a tradesperson on a managed
job. Nothing anywhere else in this repository creates that relationship: the
client terms say the tradesperson is Yaadly's subcontractor, and until this
document exists and is signed, the only paper saying so is a webpage Yaadly
wrote about itself. That is the gap this fills.

It follows the same convention as `docs/terms.html`: **what is settled is set
out in full, and what is genuinely open is a marked box rather than a plausible
paragraph nobody has checked.** There are six marked boxes. They are collected
at the end as well as appearing in place. Written wording over an open point
would read exactly like checked wording, which is worse than a visible gap.

**Four clauses need an adviser before this goes to anybody:** clause 5
(substitution), clause 11 (whether the shortfall day rate is an unenforceable
penalty), clause 16 (not going around Yaadly) and clause 24 (governing law).
Each is marked. The rest is a description of how Yaadly already works, written
down, and an adviser tightening it is a different job from an adviser deciding
it.

**The money terms at clauses 10 and 11 are settled and are not open points.**
£25 for attending a site that is not as described. Where the work is not done to
the agreed scope, the agreed price is not earned, and the fallback is the day
rate for the trade carried on the engagement note, with no figure fixed in this
agreement. Founder's decision, 5 September 2026, put twice.

**It is written to be read by the person signing it.** A tradesperson in
Portmore on a phone. Short sentences, second person, no defined-term
apparatus. That is not a softer version of a real agreement, it is the
enforceable version: terms a signer could not reasonably understand are the
first ones a court sets aside.

## Part 1. What this says, in short

This page is a summary. The agreement is Part 2, and if the two ever disagree,
Part 2 governs.

- **You work for yourself, not for Yaadly.** You run your own business on your
  own TRN. There is no rota, no payslip, no set hours, and no promise of work.
- **Each job is its own agreement.** This document sets the rules that apply
  every time. The job itself, the price, the stages and the dates, is agreed
  separately in writing before you start, in an engagement note.
- **You set your price. Yaadly engages you at that price less 12%** (10% on
  remote digital work), and you see both figures before you accept anything.
  Materials carry no fee at all.
- **Yaadly pays you, not the client.** You have no contract with the client and
  you never chase the client for money.
- **You are not waiting on a client abroad to press a button.** Yaadly checks
  your evidence against the agreed scope and pays you. Whether a client in
  London has opened WhatsApp is Yaadly's problem, not yours.
- **You never fund the client's materials out of your own pocket.** On most jobs
  materials are a stage the client has already paid Yaadly for, and Yaadly
  releases that money to you before you buy. On some jobs the client supplies
  the materials themselves and you are engaged for labour only. Your engagement
  note says which, before you start.
- **You file evidence at every stage.** Arrival, before, materials receipts,
  after, and a walk-round at the end. A stage without its evidence does not
  close and does not pay.
- **If the site is not what the client described, you can walk away** and you
  are still paid £25 for turning up. That protection is yours, not only the
  client's.
- **If work is wrong, you come back and put it right at your own cost.** Yaadly
  carries the client's claim, and Yaadly carries the money, but the making good
  is yours.
- **If a review finds the job is not finished to the agreed scope, you are told
  first and you get the chance to go back.** Go back and finish it and you are
  paid the full agreed price. Only if you will not go back, or it cannot be put
  right, are you paid a day rate for the days you actually worked instead. That
  day rate is on your engagement note before you start, and it is a floor: you
  are never paid less than it for a day you worked. Yaadly buys your materials
  at the receipted price either way.
- **Your Yaad Score is built only from finished jobs a named person approved.**
  No software ever moves it. You can see it and you can challenge it.
- **Either of us can end this** on notice. Jobs already started get finished.

## Part 2. The agreement

### 1. Who this is between

**Yaadly**: Yaadly Ltd, a company registered in England and Wales, company
number 17358077, with its registered office at [ADDRESS TO BE INSERTED, this
is the same gap already marked on `docs/terms.html`].

**You**: the tradesperson named in the signature block, trading on your own
account under your own TRN.

### 2. What this agreement is

This sets the terms that apply to every managed job you take with Yaadly. It is
not itself a job and it does not give you one.

Yaadly is under no obligation to offer you work, and you are under no obligation
to accept any work offered. You may work for anyone else, including Yaadly's
competitors, at any time, and you do not need to tell us. Neither of us is
promising the other anything until a specific job is agreed under clause 3.

### 3. How a job is agreed

For each job, Yaadly issues you an **engagement note** setting out the scope,
the price, the stages, the dates, the site and what evidence each stage needs.
Schedule 1 lists what it contains.

The engagement note is the contract for that job. Nothing starts until you have
confirmed it. Confirmation is by the code sent with it, not a click, and the
code is checked against the current version of the pack at the moment you
confirm.

**If anything in an issued pack changes, it becomes a new version and both
sides confirm again.** A code from an older version fails, and it fails with a
plain reason on the screen rather than quietly confirming something that moved
after you read it. You are never bound to a version you did not see.

### 4. You are in business on your own account

You are an independent contractor. You are not Yaadly's employee, not its
worker, not its agent, and not its partner. Specifically:

- You use your own tools and your own transport. Yaadly does not supply them.
- You decide how to carry out the work. Yaadly sets what the finished result
  must be and what evidence to file, not how you hold the trowel.
- You work the hours you choose within the dates agreed in the engagement note.
- You pay your own tax and your own contributions, on your own TRN. Yaadly
  deducts no tax from what it pays you and issues no payslip.
- You may take other work at any time.
- There is no rota, no minimum, and no continuing engagement between jobs.

You will tell Yaadly promptly if your TRN or your registered trading details
change.

> **Open point 1, for the adviser: whether the substance matches the label.**
> Writing "independent contractor" does not make somebody one. A court looks at
> control, integration and economic reality, not the heading. The genuinely
> arguable features here are the evidence obligations at clause 9, the standard
> setting at clause 11 and the panel removal at clause 20, all of which look
> like control. The counter is that they are quality specification of a result,
> which a principal is entitled to specify of any subcontractor, and that
> everything characteristic of employment is absent. This needs a Jamaican
> employment law view before anyone signs.

### 5. Doing the work yourself

Yaadly engaged you because of who you are. You were identity checked, your TRN
was matched to your ID, and your references were telephoned. That check is the
product.

So you carry out the work yourself. You may bring your own labourers or
apprentices, whom you pay and remain responsible for, but you may not pass the
job to another tradesperson without Yaadly's written agreement first, and any
substitute must pass the same checks you did.

> **Open point 2, for the adviser: this clause cuts against clause 4.**
> A genuine and unfettered right of substitution is one of the strongest
> indicators of self-employment, and this clause deliberately restricts it,
> because the vetting is the whole product and a platform that lets an
> unchecked stranger onto a client's property has sold nothing. The two aims
> are in real tension and this draft resolves it in favour of the vetting.
> That is a founder decision as much as a legal one. An adviser should say how
> much status risk it actually carries and whether a narrower wording buys
> the safety back.

### 6. What you are paid

**You set your labour price.** Yaadly engages you at that price less 12%. On
remote digital work the figure is 10%. Both numbers, your price and what Yaadly
pays you, appear on the engagement note before you accept it, and neither moves
afterwards unless the scope changes and you agree the change in writing.

**Nothing is deducted from money of yours.** Yaadly is not collecting a fee out
of your earnings and is not passing a client's money to you. Yaadly buys the
job from you at the agreed figure and sells it to the client separately, and
what the client pays Yaadly is not, and never was, your money. This is not a
wording preference. It is the difference between ordinary trading and handling
someone else's payments, and it is why nothing of yours passes through Yaadly.

**Materials carry no fee.** On Route A, Yaadly buys them from you at the price
on your receipt, adds nothing and takes nothing off. On Route B you supply no
materials and your price is your labour alone. See clause 8.

**There are no other deductions and no other charges.** You pay nothing to
join, nothing to quote, nothing for a lead you did not win, and there is no
subscription.

### 7. When you are paid

You are paid stage by stage, against the stages in the engagement note.

A stage becomes payable when the evidence for that stage is filed and a named
person at Yaadly has checked it against the agreed scope.

**Your payment does not wait on the client.** The client separately accepts the
work under their own contract with Yaadly. That is a different act, with a
different party, and it is not a condition of your money. If a client abroad
goes quiet, that is Yaadly's exposure and Yaadly's problem to carry.

The one exception is clause 19: while a complaint about your work is open,
nothing further is paid out on that job until it is resolved. Amounts already
paid to you are not clawed back.

> **Open point 3, for the adviser: payment days, and what happens on
> insolvency.** This draft deliberately does not state "within N days", because
> a number that is missed is worse than no number and the working capital
> position of a company that pays its subcontractor before its client pays it
> has not been modelled. A figure needs to be set, honoured, and stated here.
> The related question is what a tradesperson's position is if Yaadly cannot
> pay. Under this structure he is an unsecured trade creditor of a UK company.
> He should be told that plainly somewhere rather than discovering it.

### 8. Materials

Every job runs on one of two routes, and **which one applies is written on your
engagement note before you start.** They are not mixed on the same job.

**Route A, Yaadly supplied. This is the normal route and most jobs are on it.**

Materials are their own stage of the job Yaadly sold to the client, and the
client pays Yaadly for that stage before anything is bought. **You are never
asked to fund the client's materials out of your own pocket and then wait.**

That stage payment is part of Yaadly's price for the job. It is not a pot of the
client's money set aside for you, and there is no account with the client's
materials money sitting in it. The client bought a job that includes materials;
Yaadly then buys the materials. You are paid by Yaadly, as always.

The client nominates where materials are stored before any are bought. If no
suitable storage exists, that is recorded and the job is planned around it.

Against the materials list on your engagement note, Yaadly releases the
materials money to you, you buy them, and **Yaadly buys them from you** at the
price on your receipt, with nothing added and nothing taken off. They are part
of what you supply to Yaadly, and Yaadly supplies them on to the client as part
of the job it sold. Yaadly makes nothing on them, by choice.

This is not money of yours being refunded. It is a purchase, on the same footing
as your labour.

You then file the receipt, photographs, and a video of the materials in the
nominated place. **Until that evidence is filed, the materials are at your
risk.** After it is filed, they pass into the agreed custody arrangement for
that job.

Unfixed materials on site stay at your risk until they are built in. You keep
them secure while they are in your custody.

**Route B, client supplied. Labour only, and it changes what you are answerable
for.**

On some jobs the client buys and supplies the materials themselves, and Yaadly
engages you for your labour only. Where your engagement note says Route B:

- **You are not paid for materials, because you did not supply any.** Your price
  is your labour, and clause 6 applies to that price as normal.
- **On the day they arrive, you record what was delivered and what condition it
  was in**, with photographs, before you build anything with it. That record is
  what protects you, and it is the only thing that will.
- **You are not answerable for materials you did not buy.** If they are short,
  late, wrong, or not fit for the work, that is not your fault and not your
  cost. Tell Yaadly, stop if you have to, and the programme moves. You are not
  held to dates you could not meet because the materials were not there.
- **You do not guarantee somebody else's materials.** Your obligation under
  clause 11 is your workmanship. If the work fails because the blocks were poor
  and you said so in writing at the time, that is not defective workmanship.
- If you believe supplied materials are unsafe or not fit for purpose, **you say
  so before you build with them.** Building anyway, having seen it, puts it back
  on you.

> **For the founder, on Route B.** As drafted this sits as labour only, and the
> workmanship obligation is scoped to workmanship rather than to the finished
> result. That is the honest position: a guarantee over a wall built from blocks
> Yaadly neither chose nor bought is a promise about somebody else's goods. If
> Route B is ever to sit inside a managed job with the full guarantee attached,
> the client terms have to say plainly what the guarantee stops covering, and
> that is a client-side copy and insurance decision, not a subcontract one.

### 9. The evidence you file

At every stage you file:

- **The Arrival Log.** Timestamped proof you were on the correct site at the
  start.
- **Before capture.** Photographs of the condition you found.
- **Materials receipts and custody evidence**, per clause 8.
- **The Midnight Work-Log.** At the end of the day: what was done, with before
  and after capture.
- **A walk-round video** at the end of the job.

This is the job, not paperwork on top of the job. **A stage without its evidence
does not close and does not pay.** It is also what protects you: it is the
record that proves what you found, what you did, and what condition you left,
and it is the first thing anyone looks at if a client says otherwise.

Evidence must be of the actual work, taken at the time. Filing a photograph
from another job or another day ends this agreement immediately under clause
20.

### 10. If the site is not what was described

If you arrive and the site is materially different from what the client
described, tell Yaadly before starting. Photograph what you found.

**You may decline the job, and you are paid an attendance fee of £25 for turning
up.** It is paid to you in Jamaican dollars, at the rate stated on your
engagement note, so you are not carrying the exchange rate on it.

You are not held to a price quoted against a description that turned out to be
wrong, and you are not asked to absorb somebody else's mis-description.

Yaadly then either re-scopes and re-prices the job with you, in writing, or
cancels it.

*Founder decision of 5 September 2026. See the marked box at clause 11: the
figure is settled, how it sits against the shortfall day rate is not.*

### 11. Your work, putting it right, and work that is not finished properly

**The standard.** You carry out the work with the skill and care of a competent
tradesperson in your trade, to the scope agreed, using materials fit for the
purpose, and in compliance with any applicable Jamaican building requirements.

**If the work is defective, you return and put it right at your own cost,**
including your own labour and your own materials.

Yaadly answers to the client for the work. That is what being the principal
means, and it is why the client comes to Yaadly and not to you. It does not
move your obligation under this clause: as between you and Yaadly, defective
work is yours to make good.

**If the work is not done to the agreed scope, you do not get the agreed
price.** The price on your engagement note buys a finished result, not a day of
your time, and that is the deal any main contractor works to. Everything below
is how that is decided, not whether.

1. **A named person carries out the review.** Never software, never a timer,
   never a score.
2. **You are told what is wrong and shown the evidence before any decision
   about money is taken.** You may put your side, and it is recorded.
3. **You get the chance to go back and finish it.** Bring it to the agreed
   scope and **you are paid the full agreed price.** Fixing it is the fastest
   route to the full money, and returning never costs you the price.
4. If you will not go back, or the work cannot be brought to the agreed scope,
   you are paid **the day rate for your trade for the days you actually
   worked**, instead of the agreed price.
5. **The day rate for your trade is on your engagement note before you start.**
   No day rate figure is written into this agreement, deliberately: a number
   fixed in a contract signed today is wrong within a year, and the engagement
   note tracks the real rate for your trade instead.
6. Days actually worked are counted from your Arrival Log and your Midnight
   Work-Log. That is the evidence you already file, and it is why filing it
   protects you.
7. **You are never paid less than the day rate for days you actually worked.**
   That is a floor, not a target.
8. A named person decides, in writing, with the reason given. You may challenge
   it under clause 19, and the challenge is heard by a person.

**On Route A, materials you supplied and filed receipts for are bought from you
at cost either way.** They are a separate supply from your labour, they are
already in the client's building, and what a review decides about your
workmanship does not unbuy them. Your labour price is what the review bites on,
because your labour price is what the work was. On Route B the question does not
arise, because you supplied no materials.

> **Settled 5 September 2026, twice, and deliberately without a figure.** The
> shortfall is the day rate for the trade, carried on the engagement note per
> job. No day rate is fixed in this agreement. A flat figure was put and
> rejected: written into a signed contract it drifts out of line with Yaadly's
> own published day rate research within a year, and a subcontract paying a
> skilled tradesperson at a rate the platform itself has stopped standing behind
> is the Farrin price argument aimed the wrong way. The reasoning is in
> `DECISIONS.md`. The founder's position, stated plainly: work not done right
> does not earn the price.

> **Open point 4, for the adviser, two questions in one clause.**
> First: how long the making-good obligation lasts, and whether liability is
> capped at the job value or uncapped. `docs/terms.html` correctly refuses to
> promise a workmanship guarantee to clients before it is insured, and quotes
> are not in. This clause cannot run longer than what Yaadly promises the
> client, and should not run shorter. The three UK principals checked all give
> twelve months in their own name. Settle it with the insurance, in one
> decision.
>
> Second, and new: **the shortfall mechanism at paragraphs 1 to 8 needs
> testing as a possible penalty.** It replaces an agreed contract price with a
> lower sum on the judgment of the party that saves the money. A clause like
> that is at real risk of being unenforceable unless the substituted figure is
> a genuine reflection of the value actually received rather than a sanction.
> The structure above is built to survive that test: a real day rate for real
> days, a route back to the full price, notice and a hearing first, and a named
> person deciding. Whether it does survive is the adviser's call, not this
> draft's.

### 12. Health and safety

You are responsible for working safely: your own safety, your labourers', the
client's, and anyone else's on or near the site.

You provide your own protective equipment. You do not carry out work you are
not competent or qualified to do. Where work needs a licensed trade under
Jamaican law, you hold that licence and you show it before you start.

You stop work and tell Yaadly if you find something dangerous, including
anything structural, electrical or suspected asbestos. **You are never
penalised, on your Yaad Score or otherwise, for stopping work on safety
grounds.**

You report any accident or injury on site to Yaadly the same day.

### 13. Insurance

You hold your own public liability insurance and keep it in force for as long
as you take jobs under this agreement, and you show the certificate on request.

> **Open point 5: what level, and whether it is realistic.** Requiring cover a
> good tradesperson in Jamaica cannot actually buy would exclude exactly the
> people this platform is for, and a requirement everybody ignores is worse
> than none because it reads as covered. The honest options are a level set
> against what is genuinely available locally, or Yaadly carrying the cover for
> the panel and saying so. This is a commercial decision with a real cost
> attached, and it belongs with the clause 11 insurance question, not apart
> from it.

### 14. Client property, keys and occupied homes

You treat the client's property with care and leave the site clean and secure.

You do not enter parts of a property outside the agreed scope. You do not
photograph anything beyond what the evidence requires, and you do not
photograph people.

Jobs above £500, work inside an occupied home, and any job where you would hold
keys stay closed to you until your three references have been telephoned.

Where you hold keys you keep them secure, use them only for the job, and return
them on completion.

### 15. Confidentiality

The client's name, address, contact details and circumstances are given to you
for the job and for nothing else. You do not pass them on, publish them, or use
them for anything other than carrying out the job.

You may photograph your own finished work for your portfolio, provided nothing
identifies the client or the property.

This clause continues after the agreement ends.

### 16. Going direct

Yaadly finds the client, scopes the job, prices it, carries the risk and pays
you whether or not the client pays. Taking a client introduced by Yaadly off
the platform takes all of that and leaves Yaadly with the cost of it.

So: for **[PERIOD]** after a job, you do not contract directly with a client
you met through Yaadly for work of the same kind.

This does not stop you working for anyone you already knew, and it does not
stop a client engaging you for something unrelated once that period is up.

> **Open point 6, for the adviser: the period, and whether this survives at
> all.** A restraint has to be no wider than reasonably necessary to protect a
> legitimate interest or it is unenforceable in full, and an over-broad clause
> here does not degrade gracefully, it falls over and protects nothing. Twelve
> months is the instinct and it may well be too long against a single job.
> There is also a real question whether this belongs in the agreement at all
> or whether the answer is commercial: workers go around a platform that is not
> worth its fee, and this clause is a poor substitute for being worth it.

### 17. Not holding yourself out as Yaadly

You do not describe yourself as Yaadly staff, do not sign anything on Yaadly's
behalf, and do not agree prices, variations or timescales directly with a
client.

If a client asks you for more work, or asks you to change something, send them
to Yaadly. It protects you: an agreement made on the doorstep is not on your
engagement note, and what is not on your engagement note does not pay.

### 18. Your Yaad Score

Your Yaad Score is your record. It is built from completed jobs a named person
approved, and from nothing else.

- **No software ever moves it.** Software may assemble the information; a named
  person decides.
- **It is yours and it is portable.** It is a record of your work, and it goes
  with you.
- **You can see it,** and you can see what went into it.
- **You can challenge it.** If you think an entry is wrong, say so. A named
  person reviews it, and you get an answer with a reason.

### 19. If a client complains

Yaadly tells you what has been said and shows you the evidence.

**You get a fair chance to put it right.** Where the work is defective, that is
clause 11 and you go back.

While the complaint is open, nothing further is paid out on that job. What has
already been paid to you is not taken back.

A named person decides, on a published timeline, having heard both sides.
Software assembles the pack and never decides. You are told the outcome and the
reason for it.

### 20. Coming off the panel

Yaadly may stop offering you work at any time, and you may stop accepting it at
any time. Neither of us has to give a reason for that.

**Removing you from the panel is different, and it is not done silently.** If
Yaadly removes you, you are told in writing, with the reason, and you may
respond. A named person decides, and a named person decides any appeal.

Removal is immediate, without the above, where there has been dishonesty about
your identity or credentials, falsified evidence, theft, violence or threats,
or conduct that put somebody's safety at serious risk.

### 21. Your information

**Your identity documents never go to any model, ever.** Your government photo
ID and your selfie are held by Persona, the identity verification service, and
they never enter Yaadly's document store. Your face video is held to the same
rule. There is no setting that changes this.

**Your supporting paperwork goes to a model only if you agreed to it.** That is
your proof of address, TRN, certificates, CV and portfolio, and only where you
gave consent on the version of the wording you were shown. If you did not
answer, that counts as declining. Software reads them and writes notes for the
person deciding. The person decides.

You may withdraw that consent at any time by telling Yaadly, and it will not
affect an application already decided.

Yaadly's full privacy notice is at `yaadly.co.uk/privacy.html`, including where
data is held and which country the text model runs in.

### 22. Ending this agreement

Either of us may end it by telling the other in writing. Any job already
confirmed is finished and paid under the terms it was agreed on.

Clauses 11 (putting it right), 15 (confidentiality), 16 (going direct), 18
(your Yaad Score) and 21 (your information) continue afterwards.

### 23. Changes

If Yaadly changes these terms, you are sent the new version and asked to
confirm it. Jobs already confirmed stay on the version you agreed. **You are
never moved onto new terms by a notice you did not answer.**

This agreement is version **DRAFT-2026-09-05**. The version you agreed is
recorded against your account.

### 24. Which country's law

> **Open point, unresolved, and it is the one to settle first.** Yaadly Ltd is
> an England and Wales company. You are in Jamaica, the work is in Jamaica, and
> the property is in Jamaica.
>
> Jamaican law and the Jamaican courts is the honest answer and probably the
> right one: it is where the work is, and a clause pointing a sole trader in
> Portmore at an English court is a clause that will never be used, which makes
> it decoration rather than protection. English law is the instinctive answer
> for a UK company and it is the wrong instinct here.
>
> But it interacts with clause 4 (employment status is decided by the law of
> the place of work whatever this clause says), with clause 16 (a restraint is
> tested by the governing law), and with where Yaadly's insurance responds.
> Those need to be decided together by somebody qualified in both, and this
> draft states the question rather than picking an answer.

## The seven open points, collected

For the adviser brief, and for the founder where marked.

1. **Employment status** (clause 4). Whether the substance matches the label,
   under Jamaican law. Adviser.
2. **Substitution** (clause 5). This draft restricts it to protect the vetting
   and that cuts against status. How much risk, and can a narrower wording buy
   it back. Adviser, on a founder decision already taken.
3. **Payment days, and insolvency** (clause 7). A figure that will be honoured.
   And what the tradesperson's position is if Yaadly cannot pay. Founder for
   the number, adviser for the disclosure.
4. **Making good: how long, capped or not, and is the shortfall a penalty**
   (clause 11). The first two settle with the insurance, in one decision. The
   third is new and is the adviser's. Founder and adviser together.
5. **The tradesperson's insurance** (clause 13). What level, whether it is
   actually purchasable in Jamaica, or whether Yaadly carries it for the panel.
   Founder, with a cost attached.
6. **Going direct** (clause 16). The period, and whether the clause survives at
   all. Adviser.

Plus **clause 24, governing law**, which is not numbered with the six because
it is not a gap in a clause, it is the frame the other six are tested in, and
it decides first.

**Settled, and listed here so they are not reopened by accident:** the £25
attendance fee (clause 10), and the rule that work not done to the agreed scope
does not earn the agreed price, with the day rate carried on the engagement note
rather than fixed in this agreement (clause 11). Founder's decisions of
5 September 2026.
